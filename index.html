OBSERVABILITY SERIES

Introduction to Observability

Building strong observability fundamentals for SRE & DevOps Engineers

Introduction
01

What is Observability?

Observability is the ability to understand the internal behavior and health of a system by analyzing the telemetry data it produces.

02

Why Do We Need Observability?

Observability helps engineers understand and troubleshoot complex production systems when failures are difficult to identify using basic monitoring alone.

03

The Production Problem

Production issues can occur even when servers, containers, and infrastructure appear healthy, making deeper system visibility essential for finding the real cause.

04

Monitoring vs Observability

Monitoring tells us whether known conditions are healthy or failing, while observability helps us investigate why a system behaves unexpectedly.

05

Why Traditional Monitoring Is Not Enough

Traditional monitoring often focuses on predefined thresholds, while modern applications require deeper visibility into application behavior, dependencies, and user experience.

Observability Fundamentals
06

The Three Pillars of Observability

Metrics, logs, and traces provide different perspectives of system behavior and together form the foundation of modern observability.

07

Metrics — Introduction

Metrics are numerical measurements collected over time that help engineers understand system performance, health, and trends.

08

Logs — Introduction

Logs are timestamped records of events generated by applications, infrastructure, and services that provide detailed information about what happened.

09

Traces — Introduction

Traces follow requests across multiple services and show how requests move through distributed systems and where time is spent.

ARUNOPS KEY CONCEPT
Metrics tell us WHAT is happening • Logs tell us WHAT happened • Traces help us understand WHERE and WHY it happened.
Telemetry & Modern Systems
10

What is Telemetry?

Telemetry is the data generated and collected from systems, applications, and infrastructure to understand their behavior and performance.

11

Observability in Modern Applications

Modern applications use microservices, APIs, databases, queues, and external dependencies, making observability essential for understanding interactions across the entire system.

12

Observability in Cloud & Kubernetes

Cloud and Kubernetes environments require visibility across clusters, nodes, containers, pods, services, applications, networks, and cloud resources.

Observability & SRE
13

How Observability Helps SREs

Observability helps SREs detect incidents, investigate failures, perform root-cause analysis, measure reliability, and reduce mean time to recovery.

14

Observability & the SRE Mindset

The SRE mindset focuses on understanding user impact and system reliability rather than simply checking whether infrastructure is running.

15

Observability Workflow

An observability workflow moves from detecting an issue to analyzing metrics, logs, traces, dependencies, root cause, and recovery.

16

Questions Observability Should Answer

Good observability should help engineers determine what happened, where it happened, when it started, who was affected, what changed, and why it happened.

Real-World Production Scenario
17

Real-World Production Example

A production incident such as a slow checkout API demonstrates how metrics, logs, and traces can be combined to identify the root cause.

Wrap Up
18

Class Summary

Observability provides the visibility and telemetry needed by SRE and DevOps teams to understand, troubleshoot, and improve the reliability of production systems.
