01 — INTRODUCTION

Introduction to Observability

Observability helps us understand what is happening inside a system by analyzing the data it generates.

02 — FUNDAMENTALS

What is Observability?

Observability is the ability to understand the internal state of a system by examining its external outputs such as metrics, logs, and traces.

Simple Definition: Observability helps answer three important questions:

What is happening?   •   Why is it happening?   •   Where is it happening?
03 — MONITORING

Why Monitoring is Not Enough?

Monitoring can tell us that something is wrong, while observability provides the context needed to investigate why it is wrong.

Monitoring Observability
Tells us that something is wrong. Helps us understand why it is wrong.
Usually focuses on predefined conditions. Helps investigate unknown and unexpected problems.
Alerts on known failure scenarios. Provides deeper context for troubleshooting.
Example: CPU usage is 95% — Monitoring tells us the CPU is high. Observability helps us investigate which service, request, dependency, or workload is causing the problem.
04 — CORE CONCEPT

Observability Pillars

The three traditional pillars of observability are Metrics, Logs, and Traces.

📊 Metrics

Numerical measurements that tell us what is happening in our system over time.

📜 Logs

Timestamped records that provide detailed information about events occurring inside an application or system.

🔭 Traces

End-to-end records that show how a request travels across different services and components.

Remember: Metrics tell you what is happening, logs help explain what happened, and traces help identify where the request traveled and where it encountered problems.
